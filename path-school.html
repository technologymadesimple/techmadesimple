School Tech Path

I Want to Help My Kids with School Tech

Schools use more digital tools than ever — and parents are often expected to help at home with little guidance. These 7 lessons explain the most common tools, how they work, and how to support your child without needing to be a tech expert.

✓ Navigate Google Classroom confidently ✓ Help your child join video lessons ✓ Support homework using online tools ✓ Talk to your child about online safety
01

Understanding Google Classroom

Google Classroom is where most schools post assignments, announcements, and grades. Learn how to log in, find your child's work, and check what's due and when.

⏱ 8 min Parents & Guardians

02

Joining Video Lessons (Zoom & Google Meet)

If your child attends virtual classes or needs to join a school meeting, this lesson walks through setting up Zoom and Google Meet — including microphone and camera checks.

⏱ 7 min Parents & Guardians

03

Managing School Accounts & Passwords

Learn how school accounts work, how to help your child log in, and how to avoid losing access to important assignments.

04

Helping with Homework Using Online Tools

A guide to the tools schools commonly use for homework: Google Docs, Khan Academy, and Typing Club. Learn what each one does and how to help your child use them.

⏱ 8 min Parents & Guardians
→
05

Finding Assignments & Talking to Teachers

Learn how to find missing work, understand school platforms, and communicate with teachers online.

⏱ 8 min Parents & Guardians
→
06

Screen Time, Balance & Healthy Habits

How much screen time is too much? How do you tell if your child is doing schoolwork or playing games? Learn practical ways to set boundaries that actually work.

⏱ 7 min Parents & Guardians
→
07

Talking to Your Child About Online Safety

Age-appropriate ways to discuss online strangers, cyberbullying, and oversharing — without scaring your child or shutting down the conversation before it starts.

⏱ 7 min Parents & Guardians
→

Learn together

You don't need to have all the answers. Sitting with your child and exploring these tools together — and admitting when you don't know something — models exactly the kind of curiosity and openness you want to encourage in them.
